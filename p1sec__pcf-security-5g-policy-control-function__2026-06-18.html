英文原标题：PCF security in 5G: protecting the Policy Control Function

PCF security is about protecting the function that decides what every subscriber session is allowed to do. In the 5G core, the Policy Control Function sets the rules for quality of service, charging, traffic steering, and access and mobility behaviour. Because the PCF governs both what traffic is permitted and how it is billed, manipulating it or its inputs has direct service and revenue impact. It is the 5G successor to the 4G PCRF, and it inherits the same reason to be a target. What is the PCF and what does it control? The Policy Control Function is defined in 3GPP TS 23.503 for the policy framework, the Npcf services in TS 29.512 and related specifications, and security in TS 33.501. It provides policy decisions to other control-plane functions: session management policy to the SMF, access and mobility policy to the AMF, and UE policy to the device. Through these, the PCF controls QoS levels, charging control, and traffic handling for each session. The PCF does not work alone. It reads policy and subscription data from the UDR, it can receive policy influence from Application Functions, directly for trusted ones and through the NEF for external ones, and it interacts with charging. Each of these relationships is both a feature and an input that has to be trusted. Why is the PCF a security concern? The PCF sits at the intersection of service quality, charging, and traffic control. That gives it three exposures worth taking seriously. Application-driven policy influence The PCF accepts influence over policy from Application Functions. External applications reach it through the NEF, internal ones more directly. This is the designed way for applications to request QoS or steer traffic, but it is also an input surface. A malicious or compromised Application Function that is not properly constrained could attempt to push policy that raises its own QoS, redirects traffic, or affects how sessions are charged. The protection is strict authorization and bounded policy ranges, not blind trust in the request. Service-based interface exposure Like every 5G network function, the PCF is reached over service-based interfaces using HTTP/2. Weak mutual TLS, unvalidated or over-scoped OAuth2 tokens, and poisonable NRF discovery all expose the PCF to unauthorized callers. A function that can reach the Npcf services without proper authorization can attempt to read or influence policy decisions. Dependency on UDR data The PCF makes decisions based on policy and subscription data stored in the UDR. If that data or the link to it is tampered with, the PCF will faithfully enforce corrupted policy. The integrity of the PCF's decisions is only as good as the integrity of the data it reads.
