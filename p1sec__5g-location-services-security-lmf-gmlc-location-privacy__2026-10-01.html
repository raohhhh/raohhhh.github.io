英文原标题：5G location services security: protecting the LMF, GMLC and the location privacy chain

5G location services (LCS) are the standardised machinery a 5G network uses to compute where a device is and hand that position to an authorised requester, whether that is an emergency call centre, a lawful authority, an operator application or an external application function. The architecture is defined in 3GPP TS 23.273 and centres on two network functions: the Location Management Function (LMF), which calculates position, and the Gateway Mobile Location Centre (GMLC), which receives requests and enforces who may ask. Both sit on the service-based architecture, and both can produce the most sensitive data a mobile network holds about a subscriber. That matters because 5G positioning is far more precise than the cell-ID lookups that made SS7 location tracking notorious. Methods such as multi-RTT and downlink or uplink time difference of arrival can reach metre-level accuracy in suitable deployments. This article walks through the 5G LCS architecture, explains where the trust boundaries are, identifies the realistic abuse paths, and sets out what an operator should harden, monitor and test. It is a deep dive into one part of the broader problem covered in our article on location tracking attacks in mobile networks . How 5G location services are built 5G LCS splits location into a request plane, owned by the GMLC, and a calculation plane, owned by the LMF, with the AMF acting as the broker between them and the device. TS 23.273 defines the stage 2 architecture and its reference points, and the service operations are specified in the Nlmf, Ngmlc and Namf_Location APIs. The reference points describe the trust relationships precisely. NL1 carries location requests from the serving AMF to an LMF, for immediate location or for deferred periodic and triggered location. NL2 carries requests from a GMLC to the serving AMF for a target UE. NL5 carries requests from the NEF or other network functions to a GMLC. NL6 lets the home GMLC query the UDM for the target's privacy subscription and routing information. The Le reference point connects external LCS clients to the GMLC. The LMF gathers measurements in two directions. Towards the device it uses the LTE Positioning Protocol (LPP, TS 37.355), whose messages travel inside NAS transport through the AMF. Towards the RAN it uses NR Positioning Protocol A (NRPPa, TS 38.455), carried over NGAP between the AMF and the gNB. Positioning methods include NR enhanced cell ID, assisted GNSS, downlink and uplink TDOA, multi-RTT, and angle-based methods. The LMF combines the results into a location estimate with an accuracy and confidence, then returns it along the chain to the requester. Where the trust boundaries sit The critical trust boundary in 5G LCS is the GMLC, because it is the component that decides whether a requester is entitled to a subscriber's location, and every downstream function largely assumes that decision was made correctly.
