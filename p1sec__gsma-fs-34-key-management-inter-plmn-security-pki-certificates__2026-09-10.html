本文深入解析了 GSMA FS.34 规范，该规范旨在解决 4G 和 5G inter-PLMN（网间）安全中的密钥管理和公钥基础设施（PKI）问题。由于运营商间的信令交互依赖于 PLMN 标识符而非域名，传统的 Web PKI 模型无法直接应用。FS.34 定义了运营商之间交换证书和密钥材料的流程，以保护 SEPP（安全边缘保护代理）间的 N32 接口信令安全。 核心机制：FS.34 的信任模型基于证书颁发机构（CA）和信任锚。信任锚将受信任的根证书与授权的移动网络标识符（PLMN-ID）列表绑定，确保 CA 仅能为特定运营商签发证书。信任分发通过 GSMA RAEX 证书数据库实现，利用了运营商现有的漫游数据交换渠道。 潜在风险与特征：文章指出，该领域的安全失败多为操作性问题而非密码学缺陷。主要风险包括：证书过期会导致漫游服务直接中断；密钥轮换缺乏演练导致恢复缓慢；吊销机制未经验证；信任锚集陈旧导致继续接受已撤销的证书。此外，若部署时仅验证证书链而忽略 PLMN 授权映射，攻击者可能利用受信任的 CA 为未授权的 PLMN 签发证书，进而注入或窃听其他运营商用户的信令。

英文原标题：GSMA FS.34 explained: key management and PKI for 4G and 5G inter-PLMN security

Most discussion of 5G roaming security stops at the Security Edge Protection Proxy. The SEPP is presented, N32 is explained, PRINS and TLS are compared, and the conversation moves on. What that leaves out is the question every operator hits the moment they try to turn any of it on: where do the certificates come from, who decides which ones to trust, and what happens when one of them expires on a Sunday. GSMA FS.34 is the document that answers those questions, and it is the least read of the GSMA security specifications that operators actually have to implement. FS.34, published by the GSMA as Key Management for 4G and 5G inter-PMN Security and listed as inter-PLMN security in GSMA's own resource index, defines the process by which interconnect parties exchange the certificates and key material used to secure signalling between them. It is the operational half of the security model that FS.36 and 3GPP TS 33.501 describe architecturally. This post explains what the specification covers, how its trust model works, what it asks of operators as opposed to IPX providers, and what to verify in your own deployment. It is written for roaming, interconnect and security teams who have a SEPP in the plan or in production and need the key management story to be more than a line item. Where FS.34 sits in the GSMA security series FS.34 is the key management specification in a family of GSMA fraud and security documents that operators are expected to read together, and it is the one that turns architecture into procedure. The other documents in the series describe what to protect and how the protection should be shaped. FS.34 describes how the cryptographic material that makes the protection real gets from one company to another. Read alongside its siblings the division of labour is clear. FS.21 sets interconnect signalling security recommendations across SS7, Diameter and GTP. FS.31 defines a baseline security posture for the network as a whole. FS.19 addresses Diameter specifically. FS.36 covers 5G interconnect security guidelines and countermeasures. FS.34 is the one that says: here is how you and your roaming partner come to hold each other's public keys, and here is who is accountable for each step. The document has been revised repeatedly as 5G standalone roaming moved from trials into deployment, with version 6.0 issued in 2023, version 7.0 in 2024 and version 8.0 now published. That revision pace is itself informative. It reflects an industry that discovered, in the course of deploying the first standalone roaming interfaces, that bilateral certificate exchange does not scale to a global roaming footprint. P1 Security experts contributed to FS.34 and to FS.36 as GSMA members. Why inter-operator signalling needed its own key management document The direct answer is that inter-operator PKI has no natural authority to anchor it, and the web PKI model does not transfer.
