- 攻击事件背景 移动网络（特别是4G TDD和5G中频/毫米波）高度依赖精确的时间同步（相位对齐、频率精度等），通常依赖于基站或中心局的GNSS接收机以及通过传输网络分发时间的精确时间协议（PTP）。时间同步被视为一个重要的攻击面，攻击者可以在完全不接触移动核心网的情况下，通过干扰时间同步导致小区失步，从而降级无线电服务，且在网络运营中心看来更像是硬件故障。 - 攻击原理 攻击者通过降级GNSS接收机或干扰PTP流，破坏基站之间的相位对齐。对于TDD系统，相位失步会导致上下行时隙冲突，引发干扰、吞吐量下降甚至服务中断。由于GNSS民用信号微弱且未认证，容易受到欺骗攻击；而PTP协议在默认部署中未认证，容易受到主选举滥用、消息操纵和延迟攻击。 - 攻击详情 1. GNSS欺骗：攻击者发射比真实信号更强的伪造卫星信号，诱骗接收机时钟偏离UTC，而接收机仍报告正常锁定。这会导致单个小区漂移出相位预算，引发干扰。 2. PTP攻击： - 主选举滥用：攻击者注入具有吸引属性的Announce消息，诱使下游时钟选择恶意主时钟，从而分发错误时间。 - 消息操纵：篡改Sync、Follow_Up等PTP消息内容或更正字段，偏移时间偏移量的计算。 - 延迟攻击：利用PTP假设路径对称的漏洞，单向延迟数据包引入时间偏移。由于数据包本身是真实的，即使加密或签名也无法防范此类攻击。 - 攻击特征 此类时间同步攻击具有极强的隐蔽性，不会触发传统的安全运营中心（SOC）告警（如信令异常、认证失败或异常API调用）。其攻击特征主要表现在无线电和传输遥测数据中，例如载波聚合性能下降、MIMO干扰、小区吞吐量 collapse 以及类似无线电单元故障的告警。检测此类攻击需要跨团队（传输、无线电、安全）协作，提取并关联时间同步相关的遥测计数器。

英文原标题：Time synchronisation security in mobile networks: GNSS spoofing, PTP attacks and how to harden the timing plane

Time synchronisation is a security control in a mobile network, not just a transmission detail. Time division duplex radio, carrier aggregation, MIMO, positioning services and open fronthaul all depend on phase alignment that is traceable to UTC within a few hundred nanoseconds, and in most deployments that traceability begins at a GNSS receiver mounted on a cell site or in a central office. If an attacker degrades that receiver, or interferes with the Precision Time Protocol flow that distributes time across the transport network, they can push cells out of alignment and degrade radio service without ever touching the mobile core. Timing is therefore an attack surface, and one of the few where an attacker working purely in the radio frequency domain can produce a service impact that looks, from the network operations centre, like a hardware fault. This article looks at the timing plane the way a security team should look at it. It covers what synchronisation actually delivers in 4G and 5G, why GNSS is the structurally weakest link in the chain, how the distribution chain is built from primary reference time clocks, grandmasters, boundary clocks and Synchronous Ethernet, where the Precision Time Protocol itself can be manipulated, what 3GPP, ITU-T and O-RAN actually require, and what detection and hardening look like in practice. Timing sits between the transport team, the radio team and the security team, which is exactly why it tends to belong to nobody. Understanding it properly is part of a mature approach to RAN security . What time synchronisation actually does in a mobile network Synchronisation delivers three distinct things: frequency accuracy, phase alignment and time of day. Frequency accuracy keeps a radio's carrier where the standard says it should be, phase alignment keeps transmissions from neighbouring cells aligned so that uplink and downlink slots do not collide, and time of day gives absolute traceability to UTC for features and records that need it. These are not interchangeable. A network can be perfectly frequency locked and still be badly phase misaligned, and only the second failure breaks a TDD cell. Frequency division duplex radio needs frequency accuracy but is largely indifferent to phase, which is why legacy 2G and 3G deployments could be synchronised from the transport network alone. Time division duplex, which carries most 5G mid band and millimetre wave capacity, is different. Uplink and downlink share the same carrier and are separated in time, so neighbouring cells must agree on where the slot boundaries are. 3GPP TS 38.133 expresses this as a time alignment error limit between radios within transmission range of each other, commonly 3 microseconds for small radius cells and 10 microseconds for wide area cells, and ITU-T G.8271.1 expresses the equivalent network limit as a maximum absolute time error of plus or minus 1.5 microseconds relative to a common reference.
