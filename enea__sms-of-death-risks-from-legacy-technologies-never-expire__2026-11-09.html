SMS of Death: Risks from Legacy Technologies Never Expire Way back in 1999, the Nokia 7110 brought the spring-loaded keyboard cover to the mass market, letting mobile users feel like characters in the movie The Matrix (who actually used a modified Nokia 8110). It also introduced the world to the WAP browser and to the idea of accessing the Internet directly from your mobile phone.  WAP, the Wireless Application Protocol, bridged the gap between the ‘real’ Internet on your computer and the basic data connection possible on the handsets and cellular networks of the day. But what does an obsolete technology from the 20 th century have to do with the security of modern Android devices in 2026? Well, later in 1999 WAP Push was added to the standard, allowing WAP content to be ‘pushed’ to the handset with minimal intervention from the user, including over SMS. Just like SMS generally, that facility still exists to this day. A WAP Push Attack A flaw in how the Android OS processes the header of a WAP Push message allows an attacker to craft a specific, malformed message which on receipt will terminate an active call. It achieves this by crashing the com.android.phone process, the code that handles normal voice calls, and repeated messages can render the handset unusable. All of this occurs without any action by the recipient. The vulnerability was disclosed by independent security researcher Mark Blaszczyk, who dubbed it the “ SMS of Death .” It had already been r eported to Google earlier this year. The researcher also provided an app which initiates the message, with a very simple interface.
