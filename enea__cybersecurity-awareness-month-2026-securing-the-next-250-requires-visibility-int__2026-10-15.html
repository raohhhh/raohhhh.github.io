Cybersecurity Awareness Month 2026: Securing the Next 250 Requires Visibility into AI Traffic As organizations mark Cybersecurity Awareness Month 2026, one theme stands out for operators of critical infrastructure: “ Securing the Next 250 “ . Aligned with the United States’ approach to its 250th anniversary, the theme emphasizes the long-term protection of the systems that underpin modern society. Power generation and distribution, water treatment, telecommunications, transportation, emergency services, and government networks are all part of the critical infrastructure that must remain secure, resilient, and available not just today, but for decades to come. While the threats facing these sectors continue to evolve, one reality is becoming increasingly clear: securing the next 250 years of critical infrastructure will require organizations to gain visibility into one of the fastest-growing areas of network activity: AI communications . The Future of Critical Infrastructure Is AI-Driven AI capabilities are now embedded throughout enterprise software, cloud platforms, security tools, operational systems, and industrial workflows. Critical infrastructure operators have far less visibility into AI activity than they do into traditional applications and services. As such, AI is potentially an existential security challenge. Every AI Service Creates New Network Traffic One of the biggest misconceptions in cybersecurity is that AI security is primarily about protecting the model. In reality, AI systems are part of a much larger ecosystem of communications. Large language models, inference platforms, AI agents, retrieval systems, orchestration frameworks, cloud-based AI services, machine-learning pipelines, model APIs, and emerging agent-to-agent communication frameworks all rely on network traffic to function. Every one of these services introduces new protocols, new traffic patterns, and new opportunities for misuse. The challenge is growing rapidly. The result is an explosion of AI-related traffic moving across enterprise and operational networks. If that traffic is not visible, it cannot be secured. Why AI Protocol Detection Matters Critical infrastructure organizations cannot afford blind spots. Security teams need to know: Which AI services are being used across their networks. What sensitive data is being shared with external AI platforms. Whether shadow AI tools are operating outside policy. How AI agents interact with critical systems. Whether AI-enabled attacks are communicating with external infrastructure. Which new AI applications have appeared on the network. Answering these questions requires more than logs and dashboards. It requires inspection of the network itself.
