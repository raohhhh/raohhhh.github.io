英文原标题：N26 interface security: protecting 4G and 5G core interworking

The N26 interface is the control-plane link between the 4G MME and the 5G AMF that lets a device move between EPS and the 5G System without dropping its sessions. It carries mobility management and session context, including security context, between two core generations, and it is defined in 3GPP TS 23.501 and TS 23.502 with GTPv2-C as the protocol on the wire. From a security standpoint, N26 is where a hardened 5G standalone core meets an older 4G core, and the weaker side of that boundary defines how much protection a subscriber really has when they move. Most 5G standalone deployments still depend on LTE for coverage, voice fallback and roaming, so N26 is far from a niche interface. This article explains what N26 does, how security context is mapped between 5G and 4G under TS 33.501, which risks are specific to interworking, and how operators can assess and monitor the interface. It is a deep-dive companion to our broader comparison of 5G standalone and non-standalone security . What is the N26 interface? N26 is an optional inter-core-network reference point between the MME in the Evolved Packet Core and the AMF in the 5G Core, used to transfer mobility management and session management state when a single-registration UE moves between EPS and 5GS. It reuses GTPv2-C, the same protocol the MME uses on S10 towards other MMEs, as specified in TS 29.274. TS 23.501 describes two families of interworking. With N26, the network keeps one valid mobility management state for the UE, either in the AMF or the MME, and transfers context between them during idle mode mobility and connected mode handover. Without N26, the UE either operates in dual registration mode or relies on the combined HSS and UDM and the combined SMF and PGW-C to re-establish its sessions after a fresh attach or registration. The N26 option gives seamless handover and keeps IP addresses and sessions stable, which is why operators running voice over NR with EPS fallback, or any service sensitive to interruption, usually deploy it. N26 does not stand alone. Interworking relies on combined network functions that span both generations: the SMF+PGW-C, the UPF+PGW-U, the PCF+PCRF and the HSS+UDM. N26 is the piece that lets the two mobility anchors, the AMF and the MME, hand a subscriber across to each other. In practice that makes the MME and the AMF peers in a trust relationship, which is the key point for security. What N26 carries and why it matters N26 carries the information a core network needs to take over a subscriber from the other generation: identity, mobility context, PDN or PDU session information and, crucially, security context. Any party able to read or influence that exchange can learn about the subscriber or affect how the subscriber is protected after the move. The procedures are those familiar from GTP-C mobility signalling.
